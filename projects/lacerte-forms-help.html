Professional Tax Product Form Help

Putting the right guidance inside the tax form, at the field where accountants get stuck

Client: Intuit ProTax Group Year: 2023-Present

Overview

Lacerte is Intuit's tax software for professional accountants. I own its form-level help: the short guidance that appears right next to a field while someone is filling out a return. This page covers how I decide where that help goes, how I write it, and how it gets into the product.

The aim is to head off mistakes in the form before they turn into support calls, without burying accountants in text they don't need.

Problem Framing

Lacerte supports hundreds of federal and state tax forms across individual, partnership, corporate, and other returns. A lot of individual fields stand for complicated tax concepts, depend on eligibility rules with exceptions, and cause compliance problems later if they're filled out wrong.

We had thorough documentation outside the product. The trouble was that accountants got confused at the moment they were entering data, and a separate help site doesn't help much then. Errors got caught late in the return, support got more calls, and the same handful of fields kept generating the same questions.

The help existed. It just wasn't on the screen where accountants needed it.

My Role & Ownership

I'm a content designer on the ProTax Group Digital Delivery team, and I own Lacerte form help end to end. I decide which fields get guidance, set the content patterns and terminology, write and maintain the help, turn it into files the product can load, and work with engineering, tax development, and compliance to ship updates safely.

Identifying High-Risk Inputs

Not every field needs help, and adding it everywhere just creates noise. To decide where it goes, I look at four things:

  • How often the field comes up in support contacts
  • Whether people have misread it in the past
  • How sensitive it is from a regulatory standpoint
  • How much damage a wrong entry does later in the return

That keeps the help focused on the fields where it makes a difference.

Content Design Strategy

Writing for In-Product Context

I write form help for someone in the middle of a return, usually under deadline. It says what to enter and why it matters, and skips tax theory unless it's needed to get the entry right. The tone is plain and neutral, and it doesn't hedge.

Terminology & Pattern Consistency

With thousands of fields, consistency matters more than any single piece of help. I set up repeatable patterns for common types of guidance and standardized the terms we use for similar concepts. When two fields mean similar things, the help describes them the same way, so accountants don't wonder whether a difference in wording means a difference in the rules.

Technical Implementation

Mapping Forms to Content

Every piece of help has to attach to exactly one spot in the product. That means mapping it to a tax module (Individual, Partnership, Corporation, and so on), a jurisdiction (federal or state), a form, and the specific field. I keep those mappings in structured CSV files, one row per help access point.

Deep-Linking Strategy

When help points to a longer article, it uses anchor links that land on the exact section, not the top of a generic page. Accountants don't have to scan for the answer, and they can get back to the form quickly.

Build & Release Pipeline

The CSV files are converted to XML and handed to engineering for each product update. That gives us version control, a predictable release schedule, and a clear record of who changed what.

Cross-Functional Collaboration

Every update needs sign-off from tax subject-matter experts for accuracy, engineering for delivery, and compliance and legal for risk. The tradeoffs come up constantly: how complete the help should be versus how short, how fast we ship versus how deep the review goes, and how much each field can bend the standard pattern.

Outcomes & Learnings

What Worked

  • Treating the help as part of the product, written for the screen it appears on
  • Covering the risky fields well before trying to cover every field
  • Building patterns we can reuse every release

What I'd Improve

  • Measuring in-product usage from the start
  • Hearing from support teams sooner
  • Automating how we find new problem fields

Why This Matters for Trust & Safety

Trust & Safety work is about preventing harm, cutting down on error states, and helping people when they're unsure. Form help does a version of that. Accountants are filing returns for their clients, and a wrong entry can cost those clients money or create a compliance problem.

Good form help catches the mistake while the accountant is still on the field, before it becomes a problem for their client.
